# Age-25 Profile Spec

The ending has four parts, shown in order on one scrolling page with a sticky table of
contents:

1. **{name} at 25:** the profile
2. **Same choices, different start:** the comparison
3. **What could change this:** policy connections and ways to act
4. **Sources:** every source, linked

Everything is generated by rules and templates in `profile/` from the final state. No AI
calls at runtime. Every number shown cites a source.

---

## Part 1 — {name} at 25

### 1. Headline
A one-line portrait from the job, city, and one memorable flag.
- First name only; the child has no surname.
- Examples: "Ava is a respiratory therapist in Dayton who still calls her high school
  basketball coach every month." "Mateo is a second-year associate at a consulting firm in
  Chicago, running on coffee and a recruiting pipeline he didn't know existed at 17."

### 2. Narrative paragraph (100–160 words)
"At 25, {name}…" Third person, same voice as the story. Must mention at least two
specific flags or events from the playthrough (the parent's illness, the varsity years,
the summer job) so it can't be mistaken for a generic result.

### 3. Snapshot
| Field | Derived from |
|---|---|
| Education | `credential` and `collegeTier` (including "started but didn't finish") |
| Job | `job.title`, employer type, city |
| Income at 25 | `incomeByAge[25]`, rounded to the nearest $1,000, labeled "estimate," with the salary source |
| Student debt | `studentDebt` remaining and monthly payment |
| Earned from 18 to 25 | Sum of `incomeByAge` |
| Where {subj} lives | Job city + `region` flag; note if living with family |
| Health and wellbeing | `wellbeing` band, written as a sentence |
| Relationships | Friend-group flags and recurring characters |

### 4. Income from 18 to 25 (chart)
A simple line or bar chart of `incomeByAge`, with:
- A dashed reference line for the national median for {name}'s age and education
  (`benchmarks.json`)
- Markers for career events (promotion, layoff, started grad school)
- An accessible table version below the chart

Follow the `dataviz` guidance when building it. This chart makes a key point visible:
apprenticeship and work paths often earn more early, while degree paths may catch up and
pass them later. Add one sentence of projection beyond 25, citing `cew_college_payoff`,
clearly labeled as a national average and not a prediction for {name}.

### 5. Turning points
The three moments that shaped this life most.
- **Algorithm:** for each history entry, sum the absolute stat changes, any dollar effects
  traceable to it (debt from R6, salary from D15), and later effects that depended on flags
  it set. Pick the top three, with at most one from the `start` category.
- Each is one sentence linking cause to result, tagged **Starting point**, **Choice**, or
  **Chance**: "**Chance:** {Poss} dad's illness in fourth grade meant two years of
  after-school shifts at {poss} aunt's store, and less time for homework."

### 6. Chosen, chance, or starting point
A single stacked bar showing how much of the total change in {name}'s life came from:
- **Starting point** (D1–D2 and their downstream effects)
- **Choices** (D3–D15)
- **Chance** (events, lotteries, long-shot rolls, career events)

Based on the attribution rules in `state-model.md`. Include one sentence of interpretation
and an honest caveat: "This is a model, not a measurement."

### 7. Strengths
The two highest stats, turned into plain-language strengths. No numbers.

### 8. What the model leaves out
A short, honest note: the model doesn't assign race or ethnicity, disability, immigration
status, or sexual orientation (adjust to the author's decisions in `brief.md`), and research
shows each of these shapes opportunity. Link to `oi_race` and one or two other sources.

## Part 2 — Same choices, different start

Re-run the reader's choices with the same seed, but with a different family background:
- If the reader's background was in the bottom two brackets, compare with the top bracket,
  and vice versa. Middle backgrounds compare with the bottom.
- Use the same choice ids where they're available. If a choice is unavailable for the
  other background (or newly available), pick the closest option and **say so**: "Private
  preschool wasn't affordable for this family, so Ava went to Head Start."
- Same random seed, so chance events are the same; their effects differ because of the
  resource buffer, which is the point.

Show the two lives side by side: headline, education, debt, income at 25, earnings from 18
to 25, and the first point where their paths split. On phones, stack the two lives
vertically.

## Part 3 — What could change this

Pick 3–4 policies from `policies.json` whose `connectsTo` matches this playthrough's
turning points and unavailable options. Each policy card shows:
- **What it is**, in one or two sentences
- **How it connects to {name}:** "When private preschool was out of reach at age 3, …"
- **What the evidence says**, with a source link
- **Trade-offs and open questions:** cost, who pays, what's uncertain
- **Learn more** link to a nonpartisan resource

### Take action
A short, nonpartisan block:
- Find your elected officials (usa.gov/elected-officials)
- Find your local school board and its public meeting schedule
- Read more from nonpartisan research groups (Opportunity Insights and others listed in
  `sources.md`)
- Play again with a different start

---

## Part 4 — Sources

- **Used in {name}'s story:** the unique `sourceIds` from `state.history`, research notes,
  and the policies shown, grouped by life stage, in the order they appeared.
- **Full bibliography:** every entry in `sources.json`, grouped by topic.
- Each entry: authors, year, title, publisher, and a link (new tab, `rel="noopener"`).
- Every research note and event card links to its entry on this page as well as to the
  external URL.
- A link to the sources page is always available from the menu, not only at the end.

---

## Actions (bottom of the page)
- Play again (new name, new seed)
- Play again with the same background
- Let chance decide the background
- Copy a text summary to share (no personal data)

## Career fields
| Field | Example roles (lower to higher credential) |
|---|---|
| Health | Medical assistant · EMT · Registered nurse · Physical therapist · Medical student |
| Technology | IT support · Web developer · Software engineer · Data scientist |
| Business | Retail manager · Sales representative · Accountant · Consultant |
| Skilled trades | Apprentice electrician · Welder · HVAC technician · Small contractor |
| Education | Teacher's aide · Coach · Teacher · Graduate student |
| Creative | Freelance designer · Musician · Video editor · Writer |
| Public service | Service member · Firefighter · Police officer · Nonprofit staff · Policy analyst |

Within a field, the role depends on `credential` first, then `collegeTier`, `network`,
and `academics`. Salaries come from `salaries.json`. The profile never says "unemployed"
without context; if {name} is between jobs, the narrative explains why.

## Stat bands (for writing)
| Band | Range | Writing guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Low | 0–34 | Honest about the difficulty, always with something {name} has going for {obj} |
| Middle | 35–64 | Ordinary life with ups and downs |
| High | 65–100 | Strong, without implying {name} is simply better |

**Ava is a respiratory therapist in Dayton who still calls her high school basketball
coach every month.**

At 25, Ava works twelve-hour shifts at the regional hospital, three days on, four off. She
started at community college because it was close and cheap, and she kept working at the
grocery store until her second year, when Coach Daniels connected her with a hospital
volunteer program. She lives twenty minutes from where she grew up. Her mom brags about her
to people who didn't ask. The student loans are almost gone.

*Income at 25: [$X] · Debt: [$X] · Turning points:* **Choice:** a part-time job at 16.
**Chance:** a coach who knew someone at the hospital. **Starting point:** with no family
savings, community college was the only option that didn't require loans.

---

**Mateo is a software engineer in Austin, well paid and still working out what's next.**

At 25, Mateo has had two job titles and three apartments since graduating from the state
flagship. The private middle school his parents stretched to afford gave him a head start
in math and a friend group he mostly lost touch with. A summer internship arranged through
his mom's former colleague turned into his first offer. He makes more than both of his
parents did at his age, and he calls home less than he'd like.

*Income at 25: [$X] · Debt: [$X] · Turning points:* **Starting point:** parents with
degrees and a professional network. **Choice:** AP-heavy courses. **Chance:** an
internship through a family connection.

---

**Ava is a musician and part-time sound tech in Nashville, broke and mostly happy.**

At 25, Ava plays gigs four nights a week and runs sound for other bands on weekends. She
skipped college, much to her father's dismay, and a gap year became three. She has no
savings, a car with 210,000 miles on it, and more close friends than will fit in her
apartment. Her brother, the accountant, does her taxes every April and sighs loudly.

*Income at 25: [$X] · Debt: none · Turning points:* **Choice:** going all in on music at
12. **Choice:** finding the kids who shared her interests. **Chance:** a parent's job loss
at 16 that ended private lessons, and the free community music program that replaced them.
